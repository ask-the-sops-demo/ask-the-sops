# Refrigerated Medication: Temperature Excursions

> FICTIONAL TRAINING DOCUMENT. Written for a software workshop. Not clinical or regulatory guidance. Do not use in practice.

## 1. Purpose
This procedure describes what staff do when the temperature of a medication refrigerator goes outside its allowed range, so that affected stock is not dispensed until its quality has been confirmed.

## 2. Scope
Applies to every medication refrigerator in the pharmacy, including the vaccine refrigerator. Applies to all pharmacists and pharmacy technicians.

## 3. Storage range and monitoring
Refrigerated medications are stored between 2 °C and 8 °C. Each refrigerator has a digital data logger that records the temperature every 5 minutes and keeps the minimum and maximum since it was last reset. Staff read and record the current, minimum and maximum temperatures at opening and at closing, then reset the logger.

## 4. What counts as an excursion
A temperature excursion is any reading below 2 °C or above 8 °C, for any length of time. A door left open during restocking still counts if the logger records a reading outside the range.

## 4.1 Excursions during opening hours
If an excursion is noticed while the pharmacy is open:
1. Close the refrigerator door and check the power supply.
2. Do not move stock to another refrigerator unless that refrigerator is confirmed to be within range.
3. Record the time, the highest or lowest temperature reached, and how long the excursion lasted.
4. Tell the pharmacist on duty right away.

## 4.2 Overnight excursions
If the logger shows at opening that an excursion happened while the pharmacy was closed:
1. Do not dispense any product from the refrigerator. Quarantine all stock in a labelled bin marked "Do not dispense: excursion review".
2. Download or photograph the logger record showing the minimum and maximum temperatures and the duration.
3. Move quarantined stock to a refrigerator that is confirmed to be within range, keeping it separate from other stock.
4. Notify the pharmacy manager by 10:00 the same morning.
5. Contact each manufacturer for stability guidance before any product is released for use.

## 4.3 Vaccines
Vaccines follow section 4.2, and the pharmacist also reports the excursion to the regional immunization office using its excursion report form. Vaccines are not used or discarded until the office replies.

## 5. Releasing or discarding stock
Stock is released only when the manufacturer confirms in writing that it remains usable. The pharmacist records the decision and the manufacturer's reference number. Stock that cannot be confirmed is discarded as pharmaceutical waste.

## 6. Documentation
Every excursion is recorded in the temperature excursion log, with the logger record attached. Records are kept for at least two years.
