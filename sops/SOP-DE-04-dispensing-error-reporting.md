# Dispensing Error and Near-Miss Reporting

> FICTIONAL TRAINING DOCUMENT. Written for a software workshop. Not clinical or regulatory guidance. Do not use in practice.

## 1. Purpose
This procedure makes sure dispensing errors and near misses are reported, reviewed and learned from, without blame.

## 2. Definitions
A dispensing error is a mistake that reaches the patient, such as the wrong drug, strength, dose, directions or patient. A near miss is a mistake that is caught before the product leaves the pharmacy.

## 3. Immediate steps after an error
1. Make sure the patient is safe. If there is any risk of harm, the pharmacist contacts the patient and their prescriber right away.
2. Retrieve the incorrect product if possible.
3. Tell the pharmacy manager the same day.

## 4. Reporting
Every dispensing error and near miss is reported in the incident log within 24 hours, by the person who found it. The report describes what happened, not who is to blame.

## 5. Look-alike and sound-alike medications
Some drug names look or sound alike and are easy to confuse, for example hydroxyzine and hydralazine. To reduce the risk:
1. Store look-alike pairs on separate shelves, never side by side.
2. Use tall-man lettering on shelf labels: hydrOXYzine and hydrALAZINE.
3. Always confirm the product by scanning the DIN on the stock bottle against the prescription. For example, the training product "Sampleazine 10 mg" has DIN 99990001 (a fictional number used only in this training document).

## 6. Review
The pharmacy manager reviews all reports each month and shares the lessons learned with the team.
