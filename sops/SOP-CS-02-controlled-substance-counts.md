# Controlled Substance Counts

> FICTIONAL TRAINING DOCUMENT. Written for a software workshop. Not clinical or regulatory guidance. Do not use in practice.

## 1. Purpose
This procedure keeps an accurate, checked record of every controlled substance in the pharmacy, so that losses and errors are found quickly.

## 2. When counts happen
A full count of all controlled substances is done every Monday before opening. A partial count of the affected products is done whenever an order is received, a product is returned to the supplier, or a discrepancy is suspected.

## 3. Who counts
Counts are always done by two people: a pharmacist and a pharmacy technician, or two pharmacists. A pharmacy technician may not do a count alone. Both people sign the count record.

## 4. How to count
1. Count each product by strength and form, one product at a time.
2. Count unopened containers by the seal, and open containers by the unit.
3. Compare each count with the running balance in the controlled substance register.
4. Both people initial each line before moving to the next product.

## 5. Discrepancies
If a count does not match the register:
1. Recount immediately, with both people present.
2. If it still does not match, check the week's prescriptions, receipts and returns for recording errors.
3. If the discrepancy cannot be explained within 24 hours, the pharmacy manager reports it as a loss using the loss report form, and records the report number in the register.

## 6. Storage
Controlled substances are kept in the locked cabinet. The cabinet key is held by the pharmacist on duty and is never left in the lock.

## 7. Records
Count records and loss reports are kept for at least two years.
